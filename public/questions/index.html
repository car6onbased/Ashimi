The designer in this world.

I’m Ashimi Adefesobi, a Senior / Lead Service Designer based in London. My work spans public services, financial services, higher education and commercial organisations.

This world is a way into my practice. The case studies show the work behind it.

Understand people. Make the service visible.

I look across the experience and the organisation delivering it: people, policy, processes, technology and the handoffs between them.

I bring those perspectives together, test ideas and use the evidence to decide what should change.

FAQs and interviews

Common questions about my work, plus a few examples I can talk through in an interview.

Frequently asked questions

What do you do as a service designer?

I look at how a service works across the people using it, the teams running it and the systems they depend on. I make the gaps visible and work with the people who own them to decide what to change and how to test it.

Where should I start with your work?

Takumi shows a change to a live platform. Richmond shows how council services look from the resident’s side. The simulations are fictional and let you try some of the decisions that come up in that work.

How can I get your CV or arrange a conversation?

Email me with a little about the role or project and what you’d like to discuss.

Interview questions

How do you respond when the brief already names the solution?

I ask what people need to be able to do and where the existing service makes that hard. At Takumi, that meant looking at how clients decided on creators and why they still needed spreadsheets to make the decision. Read the Takumi case study.

How do you find out what happens between teams?

I trace the same journey from several sides. At the Department for Education that meant looking at the learner’s journey alongside the work of employers, training providers and awarding bodies. Read the apprenticeship case study.

How do you turn a service map into a tool people can use?

At Pertemps I used the recruitment journey to organise the CRM around what recruiters had to do next. That included improving the data they used to match candidates and making progress visible. Read the recruitment case study.
